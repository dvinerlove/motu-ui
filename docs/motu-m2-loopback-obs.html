MOTU M2 / M4 loopback for OBS, Discord and recording

The M-series driver gives you loopback channels that the stock panel barely explains. Here's what they are and how to use them without feedback loops.

The three capture devices

With the MOTU driver installed, Windows sees three recording devices:

In 1-2 Your physical inputs (mic, guitar, line)
Loopback Whatever the computer is playing — DAW output, browser, game, Spotify
Loopback Mix Computer output plus your physical inputs, already summed

In OBS

  1. Add an Audio Input Capture source → pick MOTU M Series Loopback to capture what your PC plays.
  2. Add a second source → MOTU M Series In 1-2 for your mic.
  3. Keep them as separate sources — you get independent faders and can mute the desktop feed on stream without touching Windows.
If you only want one OBS source and don't need separate faders, use Loopback Mix — mic and desktop audio are already combined. Its mic level follows the front-panel GAIN knob, so set it carefully.

In Discord / Zoom

Pick In 1-2 as the microphone. Do not pick a Loopback device — your call partners would hear your desktop audio (and their own voice echoed back to them).

The feedback trap

Routing Loopback into software that also outputs to the M2 creates a feedback loop: the capture contains the playback which contains the capture. If you hear rising howl or echo, that's why. Monitor the M2's headphone output, not the loopback feed.

Common problems

  • Loopback is quiet — its level is the channel level of whatever plays. Raise the app/system volume; the loopback carries what you hear.
  • Nothing on Loopback Mix — check that your inputs are active and the GAIN knob isn't at zero. Mix = playback + inputs; if inputs are silent, only playback is captured.
  • Audio drops / crackles — a buffer-size mismatch between Windows and your app. Lower the buffer one step and match sample rates everywhere.
  • Can't tell if loopback carries anything — see below.

Seeing the loopback level before you record it

The stock MOTU panel has no meters for the loopback channels, so the usual debugging flow is blind: arm OBS, record a clip, play it back, guess. motu-ui is a free open-source utility that shows a live meter of the loopback endpoint next to the input and output meters — the speaker button sends a 440 Hz tone through the device so you can watch OUT and LOOP light up and know the path works before you hit record.
